THE PROBLEM

A modern Indian retailer runs a mixed fulfilment network — warehouses, city fulfilment centres, quick-commerce dark stores, and seller-owned inventory. The same SKU is routinely in the wrong place: a fast-moving essential sits two days away in a warehouse, while a fast-delivery store's scarce shelf space holds slow movers that could ship from anywhere. Placement is decided location-by-location by different teams optimizing different things off weekly reports — nobody holds a network-level view balancing holding cost, transfer cost, and the sales lost every time a customer sees "not deliverable."

YOUR MISSION

Build an AI system that decides how much of each SKU to hold at every location in a retail network, and recommends the transfers to rebalance stock between locations — balancing demand, capacity, transfer cost, and delivery-speed promise. Split the reasoning across cooperating agents grouped by whatever real dimension your data supports — region, category, or store — that negotiate toward a network-optimal plan a human planner can approve or override.

SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

Better pincode-level product availability and a bigger serviceable catalog

Lower total holding + transfer cost at the same service level

Better in-stock rate at your fastest-turnover locations for high-velocity SKUs

Less slow-moving inventory occupying your best-performing locations

GUARDRAILS YOU MUST RESPECT

Auditability: every placement and transfer recommendation must show its demand basis and cost trade-off for planner approval.

Cost Guardrail: never recommend a transfer that costs more than the margin it unlocks; report cost-per-recommendation.

Tiered Reasoning: match compute to value — cheap models for the long tail of C-class SKUs, expensive reasoning reserved for high-value A-class SKUs.

Capacity Feasibility: respect physical capacity, cold-chain constraints, and category restrictions — an infeasible plan scores zero.

Human-in-the-loop: bulk transfer plans above a value threshold require planner sign-off before execution.

GENERAL BUILD PRINCIPLES (APPLIES TO EVERY TRACK)

Prefer several specialized, cooperating agents over one giant mega-prompt.

Use cheap/fast models for routine decisions and save expensive reasoning for the high-stakes ones — report a rough cost-per-decision.

Where practical, show that a smaller open-source model, RAG, or a classical solver could replace constant calls to an expensive commercial LLM.

Add a self-check step: have the system review its own output against the Business Goals before it's final.

Benchmark against a strong classical solver and show exactly where your AI adds value over it — inventory placement is one of the tracks where you're expected to beat a classical baseline, not just approximate it.
